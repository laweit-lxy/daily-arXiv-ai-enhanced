Motivation: 宇宙射线 μ 子可被空间分离的多个设备独立探测，同一 μ 子事件可各自衍生出对应的随机比特段，因此可作为天然的共享随机源，用于设备间同步随机比特生成（如安全通信中的密钥协商）。关键难点在于识别共同 μ 子事件并正确对齐事件序列。

Method: 提出 MuSTRNG 系统，含两种同步方案。(1) Tracking–Tracking：三级筛选——基于径迹的筛选剔除重建径迹不经过对方探测器区域的事件；基于角度的筛选用角度分箱对齐事件序列、剔除不一致对；基于时间戳的筛选比较高阶时间戳位以抑制残余失配。(2) Tracking–Nontracking：仅在具备径迹能力的设备端做径迹筛选，再在两设备间做时间戳筛选。

Result: 用两台具备径迹能力的设备构建原型评估：Tracking–Tracking 三级流程在 15 比特随机段下将无效事件比例降至 0.28%，生成的比特序列在适用的 NIST SP 800-22 测试中总体表现良好；将一端数据去除径迹信息后重处理，为 Tracking–Nontracking 提供初步支持，5 比特随机段下无效事件比例为 0.30%。

Conclusion: 结果证明在不同设备配置下基于 μ 子的同步随机比特生成是可行的，并可应用于安全通信等场景。

Abstract: Cosmic-ray muons provide naturally occurring stochastic events detectable by spatially separated devices, offering a potential physical source for synchronized random-bit generation. This is possible because the same physical muons can be independently detected by both devices and used to derive corresponding random-bit segments. Therefore, identifying common muon events and correctly aligning the event sequences are essential for synchronized random-bit generation. This article presents MuSTRNG, a muon-based synchronizable true random number generation system with two synchronization methods: Tracking--Tracking and Tracking--Nontracking. For Tracking--Tracking synchronization, track-based screening removes events whose reconstructed tracks do not intersect the detector area of the other device; angle-based screening partitions reconstructed directions into angular bins and aligns the event sequences to remove inconsistent pairs; and timestamp-based screening compares higher-order timestamp bits to further suppress residual mismatches. Tracking--Nontracking synchronization applies track-based screening only at the tracking-capable device, followed by timestamp-based screening between the two devices. A prototype with two tracking-capable devices was evaluated. For Tracking--Tracking synchronization, the three-stage process reduced the invalid-event ratio to 0.28% using 15-bit random segments, while the generated bit sequences showed generally favorable results in the applicable NIST SP~800-22 tests. Reprocessing the data without track information from one device provided preliminary support for Tracking--Nontracking synchronization, yielding an invalid-event ratio of 0.30% using 5-bit random segments. These results demonstrate the feasibility of muon-based synchronized random-bit generation under different device configurations, with potential application to secure communications.
